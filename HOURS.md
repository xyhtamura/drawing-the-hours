# Visits, inhabitants, and borrowed time

**Naming update, 2026-09-13:** Xyh proposed Drawing the Hours and requested
immediate separation from a LOOP remake. The [working scope](SPEC.md) adopts
that title and records the independent design brief. Earlier options below
are retained as history; Cradle is no longer the working title.

Ideation recorded on 2026-09-13. **Cradle**, **String figures**, and **Draw them
together** are Xyh's tentative naming directions. The folder remains
`relation-garden`; no title has been selected. This note extends and amends
the [earlier daytime concept](CONCEPT.md), especially its restriction of moths
to daytime shade. Dawn, noon, dusk, and midnight are now possible settings.

## Xyh's additions

Xyh wants a clearer identity beyond LOOP: different insect kinds and names,
distinct behaviors, plants and airborne seeds, and strange quiet hours in the
same meadow. They name *Abney & Teal* and Ray Bradbury's *Dandelion Wine* as
associations. These are references to investigate through Xyh's preferences,
not instructions to reproduce either work's characters or visual treatment.

The narrative might involve someone who has school or work and can visit only
between obligations. Noon, dawn, dusk, and midnight could provide those gaps.
The child's free afternoon, perhaps 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., remains another possible
frame. Protagonist age, literal versus imagined visits, and chronology are open.
The idea that outdoor childhood has changed is Xyh's reflection, not a
historical finding established in this note.

Xyh recalls LOOP's sun and moon tracing time during ordinary and bonus stages
and asks for a different timer. They also ask whether an unavailable game's
recreation can be justified as an homage. LOOP's retrieved rules and present
playability have not been verified here.

## A distinction that changes play

Agent proposal: build around visits to one familiar meadow whose inhabitants
and opportunities vary with the hour. Keep the earlier string-figure grammar:
the drawing can separate groups, relate their contents, and touch selected
objects. The player's knowledge must include both the figure and the behavior
of what they are trying to put inside it.

Different insect names should correspond to different decisions. Merely
replacing three butterfly colors with three insect pictures would preserve
the same game under different labels. Candidate behaviors for a fictional
prototype include hovering followed by a dash, landing and taking off,
ground-bound travel, and drifting with wind. Each changes when and where a
loop can close, or whether an unfinished border helps or obstructs grouping.
Actual insect names require a regional and biological check before these
behaviors are presented as educational facts.

Airborne seeds add objects whose movement and role can differ from insects.
A figure could place insects in one pocket and seeds in another, then trigger
a dispersal pattern on release. Fixed plants could provide a landing place or
an anchor for a figure. These are proposed game relationships, not botanical
claims. Keep a small initial cast in which every additional kind earns a
different handling rule.

The earlier vocabulary of catching remains available, but the outcome need
not always be capture. Gathering, separating, sheltering, releasing, and
splitting suitable objects can form different recipe families. Decide outcomes
explicitly before scoring them.

## Four hours, one place

Agent proposal: retain recognizable landmarks across visits so a change in
sound, available space, or population is noticeable.

| Visit | Proposed scene | Proposed play distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn | Paper fibers catch a low light; dew-like highlights mark the ground. | A sparse cast leaves space to learn precise figures. |
| Noon | Bleached grass, hard fence shadows, a nearly empty park. | Wind-driven seeds and crossing traffic interrupt apparent stillness. |
| Dusk | Pink-gray paper, reduced contrast, a distant window becoming lit. | Two differently moving groups overlap during a handover. |
| Midnight | A localized pool of lamplight with the meadow continuing beyond it. | A smaller visible field changes enclosure decisions. |

These are authored scene ideas, not descriptions of actual species' activity.
Noon is a threshold in the character's schedule rather than a twilight in the
sky. Its stillness can be as unusual as midnight. The nighttime palette can
remain delicate and playful, with lavender, cream, faded pink, and ink blue.

An unobtrusive opening caption could be "Before school", "Lunch break", or
"Can't sleep". Pick a consistent life for the character before using these as
a sequence. A bag at the edge, a route beyond the fence, or a distant signal
could establish the obligation without a visible avatar or explanatory plot.
The player retains the ability to return; leaving the meadow is not abandonment.

Visits are game scenes, not real-world clock restrictions. A player must not
have to open the game at midnight to see a midnight level.

## Timer candidates

| Candidate | What changes | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dandelion head | A fixed number of visible seeds depart as visit time expires. | Fits the plant motif; must remain distinct from seeds participating in play. |
| Approaching departure | A small timetable strip counts toward a bus, bell, or other commitment. | Makes the narrative concrete but decides more about the character. |
| Unwinding spool | A cord gradually leaves a spool at the edge. | Connects to string play but can be mistaken for a limit on stroke length. |

Agent preference: try the dandelion head as the common clock, with the reason
for departure supplied by the scene. Seed loss represents elapsed simulation
time; it is an invented interface convention, not a natural timekeeping claim.
Show a discrete remaining count or optional numeric countdown, pause it with
the game, and announce the final interval. Ambient changes alone are too
ambiguous to explain a deadline. At zero, stop spawning and give an active
stroke a short, visibly bounded completion window; its duration is unchosen.

The sun and moon can remain scenery without measuring each round. This separates
the hour of the visit from the remaining duration of the visit. The four
settings therefore need not depict four accelerated days or nights.

## Name and framing

**Cradle** joins string figures, holding things briefly, and a childlike register.
An existing game uses that exact title: Flying Cafe for Semianimals' game is
listed by [Steam support](https://help.steampowered.com/en/wizard/HelpWithGameTechnicalIssue?appid=361550).
That is a searchability collision, not by itself a legal determination. No
trademark clearance search was performed. Keep the name provisional.

**String figures** names the player's action and leaves the inhabitants open.
**Draw them together** could be a subtitle or opening phrase. **How to catch
butterflies**, which Xyh also floated, could title the first notebook page:
later pages could extend its initial idea of catching to other relationships.
These are distinct naming roles to consider, not a selected title hierarchy.

## Homage, authorship, and rights

Narrow legal orientation, checked against U.S. official sources on 2026-09-13;
not a clearance opinion for this game or for Philippine or other law.

The [U.S. Copyright Office's game guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
distinguishes game ideas and methods of play from protected literary and
pictorial expression. A new implementation of a gesture mechanic is a
different question from copying artwork, rulebook prose, music, or other
expressive material. Renaming a recreation does not itself resolve that issue.
The Office's [copyright overview](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/)
describes permission, applicable exceptions, and the public domain as routes
for reuse; unavailability and an homage label do not themselves establish any
of those. No rights-holder, expiry, or license investigation of LOOP was made.

Names also raise a separate trademark question. The
[USPTO's guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)
considers confusingly similar marks together with related goods or services.
Calling a game an homage does not establish permission or official affiliation.

Ethical design recommendation: credit LOOP explicitly as an influence, author
the assets, wording, levels, and implementation, and make the proposed
differences matter to play. An acknowledgment can name what was learned from
it without presenting the new game as its official successor. Preservation of
LOOP and creation of this game are separate projects. The inability to play a
work can motivate preservation without settling rights to its content.
