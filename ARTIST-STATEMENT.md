# Drawing the Hours: working artist statement

Working draft for SOMEWAREZ / TOUCH-SCREEN, saved 2026-10-24 from the conversation with Xyh. Not final exhibition copy. Further material and revision questions are in [local conversation notes](ARTIST-STATEMENT-NOTES.local.md), which are gitignored and available only in this checkout.

## Draft

*Drawing the Hours* is a drawing game set among insects and plants in the small spaces of a working day. Its speaker waits for transport, takes a lunch break, brings home medicine, and tries to sleep. During these intervals, the player draws cords around moving creatures, threads them in sequence, and learns combinations.

I remember visiting my mother’s office and seeing a flower doodle among the papers she had to sign and the ledgers she had to look at. That memory returns when I think about the game. Its speaker’s circumstances draw on working people around me, from my childhood and among my peers. The places are closer to my own experience of living in Tandang Sora, Quezon City, as the area developed. Roadside plants and insects occupied patches between roads and buildings. I wanted to bring these overlooked ecologies into view and make their movements something a player could think with.

The game began with the looping mechanic of the Flash game *Loop*. *Fruit Ninja* informed its cutting and threading gestures, while *Candy Crush* was another reference for its sequence of short levels. I was interested in games that fit into waiting and travel, that someone can pick up, pause, and return to as circumstances allow. The tablet presentation connects these habits of play to the intervals described by the speaker.

I also wanted the childlike appearance to hold elaborate rules. Childhood games could become complicated, and learning their rules was part of the pleasure. The cords suggest cat’s cradle, sewing, and needlework, activities whose associations with feminine play interest me. A stroke can accidentally thread a creature or close around an unwanted one. Playing asks for speed at some moments and patience at others, along with care, precision, and decisions about what to attempt next.

There is something almost magical about learning a figure that produces a particular result. Drawing can also change how the surrounding situation feels. I think of watching raindrops combine on a windowpane, then imagining how I might bring them together myself. Among the game’s insects, that impulse becomes a temporary arrangement made through gesture. It might offer pleasure, relief, or a way to think during a difficult day. The flower among my mother’s papers leaves those possibilities open.
